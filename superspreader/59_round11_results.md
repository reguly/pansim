## Headline

1. **Worlds 1, 2a and 2b are matched on the negative-binomial likelihood estimator (`k_ml` 0.357, 0.355 and 0.365, within 3% of each other and within 4.4% of the 0.35 target, with overlapping 90% intervals), and at matched likelihood k they also match the observed body of the offspring distribution.** Their observed zero shares are 56.6% (56.0 to 57.2), 52.8% (52.1 to 53.6) and 53.5% (53.1 to 54.1) (within 0.04, 1.3 and 1.1 percentage points of the negative binomial at each arm's own likelihood k and mean: 56.5%, 54.1%, 54.6%), and the observed shares of infectors causing 80% of infections are 21.5%, 21.1% and 21.7% (negative binomial: 21.4%, 21.5%, 21.8%).
2. **All six registered predictions (a) to (f) in R11-1 hold.** Matching on the likelihood k rather than the moment k preserves every discriminator found in round 10 and sharpens two of them: the ratio of likelihood k to moment k cleanly separates the multiplier-only world (1.06) from both scene-plus-multiplier worlds (1.5 in world 2a and 1.42 in world 2b), and the far tail now separates them as well (largest offspring 88 in world 2a and 57 in world 2b, replicate mean 37.6 and 36.8, against 54 and 27.7 in world 1), because matching the body on likelihood k forces the scene worlds to carry a lower moment k (0.236 and 0.257).
3. **World 2b (moderate scene, lambda 10) reaches likelihood k 0.365 at multiplier shape 0.58.** It does not need a shape near or below 0.32 (world 1's shape), because the log-log slope of likelihood k against shape steepens below shape 1 (`59a` item 2). A moderate scene (`lambda = 10`, `tau = 1`) cuts the required squared coefficient of variation of the per-episode multiplier from 3.125 (shape 0.32) to 1.724 (shape 0.58), almost in half; the strongest scene (`lambda = 30`) cuts it to 1.429 (shape 0.70).
4. **Correction to note 58, and a reversal of the round-10 conclusion: world 3 (closed-group events) CAN be matched on likelihood k.** Note 58 says the lowest likelihood k the events have reached is 1.8 (round 6) or 1.2 (round 10) and asks me to report that events cannot be matched. That is wrong, and so was my own round-10 reading. Going back through every closed-group arm in `43_r67_closed_group_summary.csv`, the round-6 arm at group size 30 and **event rate 0.1456** reaches **likelihood k 0.330 (0.322 to 0.337)**, calibrated, 12 replicates, day 28 at +0.2%. The 1.8 figure in note 58 belongs to the group-size-100 arms; the group-size-30 arm tuned on the moment k was never read on the likelihood scale. Its observed zero share is 52.3% (51.7 to 52.9) and its observed 80% share is **18.1%**, the closest of any arm to the real 19% to 20%. The round-10 arm (rate 0.034, likelihood k 1.2) was simply at too low an event rate. I report this arm as **world 3b** throughout and score world 3 as matchable. What events still cannot do is produce a negative-binomial-shaped distribution: at its own likelihood k and mean the negative binomial predicts a 56.6% zero share against the observed 52.3%, a 4.4-point miss, three times the miss of either scene world, because the event mechanism makes the offspring distribution bimodal rather than negative binomial. Nor is the parameter modest: 14.6% of agents attend a closed group every day and 61.7% of all transmission happens at those events.
5. **R11-2 (whether the repeat-infection correlation comes from the persistent class) is settled by decomposing the correlation within and between class membership.** In world 2a (`lambda = 30`, shape 0.70), the overall Pearson correlation across 3459 repeat-infection pairs is 0.0711 (0.0431 to 0.0985; Spearman 0.0991), against -0.0122 in world 1. Computed separately *within* class members (1959 pairs) and *within* non-members (1500 pairs), the Pearson correlation drops to **-0.00628** and **-0.0152**. The same decomposition on the round-10 gate cell (overall Pearson 0.104 across 5285 pairs) gives **-0.00715** within class members and **-0.0214** within non-members. Both within-group correlations sit below 0 and well below the registered 0.03 threshold: the positive repeat-infection correlation is carried entirely by persistent class membership, not by household, home-location or age persistence.
6. **R11-3 (cluster composition in contact-tracing terms) separates all three mechanism families.** In the ten largest clusters of each replicate, the share of offspring in the infector's own household is 17.7% in world 1, 3.3% in world 2a (6.5% in world 2b, 7.3% in the round-10 gate cell), and 3.9% in world 3b (5.6% in world 3); the share in the cluster's single modal venue type is 49.2%, 41.9% (40.5%, 42.2%) and 96.8% (93.4%). Four findings stand out in the main table:

1. **Matching on likelihood k aligns the observed body of the offspring distribution across worlds 1, 2a and 2b.** In round 10, matching on moment k left an 8.8-point gap in observed zero share between world 1 (56.6%) and the gate cell (47.8%). 2. **In both scene worlds, the negative binomial at `k_ml` and the arm's own mean fits the observed zero share within 1.1 to 1.3 percentage points** (`52.8%` vs `54.1%` in world 2a; `53.5%` vs `54.6%` in world 2b) and fits the 80% share within 0.1 to 0.4 points (`21.1%` vs `21.5%`; `21.7%` vs `21.8%`). By contrast, the negative binomial evaluated at the *moment* k overpredicts the zero share by 8.4 points in world 2a (`61.2%`) and 7.0 points in world 2b (`60.6%`), because the moment estimator is pulled down by the far tail.
3. **The ratio `k_ml / k_mom` remains a sharp fingerprint at matched likelihood k**: `1.06` in world 1 against `1.50` in world 2a, `1.42` in world 2b, `2.16` in world 3b and `3.11` in world 3. The ordering is the same one round 10 found, and world 3b sits between the scene worlds and the low-rate event arm.
4. **Of the four matched worlds, the events arm is the one the negative binomial fits worst.** World 3b's observed zero share is `4.4` points below the negative binomial at its own likelihood k and mean (`52.3%` against `56.6%`) and its 80% share is `2.4` points below (`18.1%` against `20.5%`), against misses of `0.04` to `1.3` points in worlds 1, 2a and 2b. A closed-group event puts a whole group's infections on one infector on one day, which is a mixture, not a gamma-mixed Poisson. Matching the likelihood k therefore does not make the events arm negative binomial; it only makes its k comparable. Measured against the real data rather than against a fitted negative binomial, though, world 3b's observed `18.1%` causing 80% is the closest of any arm to the reported `19%` to `20%`.

1. **Prediction (e) is met on the point estimate.** Pooled across all repeat-infection pairs, world 2a has a Pearson correlation of `0.0711` (`0.0431` to `0.0985`; Spearman `0.0991`), above the `0.05` threshold (its 90% replicate bootstrap interval spans `0.05`), while world 1 sits at `-0.0122` (`-0.0267` to `-0.000444`), well below `0.02`. World 2a's overall correlation (`0.0711`) is slightly lower than the round-10 gate cell's (`0.104`) for two reasons: its per-episode gamma noise has higher variance (`1/0.70 = 1.43` vs `1/1.00 = 1.00`), and at `lambda = 30` class members are depleted faster (`1,959` of `3,459` repeat pairs, `56.6%`, are class members, with mean offspring falling from `3.16` on the first episode to `2.40` on the second).
2. **R11-2 is answered unequivocally: within class members and within non-members, the repeat-infection correlation is zero (slightly negative), matching world 1.** In world 2a, restricting to class members (`1,959` pairs) reduces Pearson r from `+0.0711` to **`-0.00628`** (`-0.0343` to `+0.0250`, Spearman `-0.00390`), and restricting to non-members (`1,500` pairs) reduces it to **`-0.0152`** (`-0.0473` to `+0.0113`, Spearman `-0.0356`). Both are well below the registered `0.03` threshold. The exact same collapse occurs in the round-10 gate cell (`lambda = 10`, shape 1): overall Pearson r is `+0.104`, but within class members (`2,295` pairs) it is **`-0.00715`** and within non-members (`2,990` pairs) it is **`-0.0214`**. Why is the pooled correlation positive when both subgroup correlations are slightly negative? Because class members average `3.16` and `2.40` offspring on their first and second episodes in world 2a (and `3.14` and `2.16` in the gate cell), whereas non-members average `0.80` and `0.64` (`0.85` and `0.66`). Mixing two internally uncorrelated subgroups whose means differ by a factor of four creates a purely between-group covariance. That proves the interpretation in note 57: the repeat-infection correlation in the scene worlds comes from persistent membership in the high-volume class, not from household size, home location, or age.

## 6. What the like-for-like match changes for the paper

1. **The conclusion carried over from note 57 has to be softened. On the like-for-like estimator, all three mechanism families reach the real level of dispersion; what distinguishes them is the shape of the distribution and of the clusters, not whether they can get there.** Note 57 concluded that "in this model only variation in infectiousness between infections reproduces the observed offspring distribution; places and events do not do it". The first half holds in a weaker form and the second half does not: at likelihood k about 0.35 we now have a multiplier-only world (`0.357`), two scene-plus-multiplier worlds (`0.355`, `0.365`) and a pure closed-group-event world with no multiplier at all (`0.330`, world 3b). The event world was missed in rounds 6 and 10 because it was only ever read on the moment scale. What remains true, and is the sharper statement, is that **the events arm is the only one whose offspring distribution is not negative binomial** (observed zero share `4.4` points below the NB at its own k and mean, against `0.04` to `1.3` points for the other three), and that it needs an implausible event rate (`14.6%` of agents in a closed group every day, `61.7%` of all transmission inside those groups).
2. **Both the moderate scene (`lambda = 10`) and the strongest scene (`lambda = 30`) reach the real pattern when paired with a sub-exponential multiplier (shapes 0.58 and 0.70).** Note 57's concern that the moderate scene might require a multiplier shape near 0.32 (leaving nothing for the scene to contribute) does not materialize: because `k_ml` steepens below shape 1, shape `0.58` suffices at `lambda = 10` (`k_ml = 0.365`, zero share `53.5%`, 80% share `21.7%`), and shape `0.70` suffices at `lambda = 30` (`k_ml = 0.355`, zero share `52.8%`, 80% share `21.1%`). The moderate scene cuts the required multiplier variance `1/shape` from `3.125` (world 1, shape 0.32) to `1.724` (shape 0.58), and the strongest scene cuts it to `1.429` (shape 0.70).
3. **Every qualitative discriminator from round 10 survives when the worlds are matched on likelihood k and the observed body shares, and world 3b gives the comparison a fourth arm with no multiplier at all:** home share (`34.2%` in world 1 vs `6.0%` and `14.1%` in the scene worlds vs `22.6%` in world 3b), high-L venue share (`16.7%` vs `91.2%` and `77.9%` vs `12.5%`), household secondary attack rate (`0.00165` vs `0.00041` and `0.00083` vs `0.0019`), ratio of likelihood to moment k (`1.06` vs `1.50` and `1.42` vs `2.16`), repeat-infection correlation (`-0.012` vs `+0.071`), and the contact-tracing structure of large clusters (household share `17.7%` vs `3.3%` to `6.5%` vs `3.9%`; single venue-day share `19.8%` vs `14.5%` to `14.7%` vs `88.2%`). Cluster *size* identifies nothing: world 3b has the largest clusters of the four matched worlds and the smallest footprint.
4. **The repeat-infection fingerprint is now explained, not just observed.** Conditioning on the agent's class flag zeroes out the repeat-infection correlation in both world 2a and the round-10 gate cell (`r = -0.006` to `-0.021`), proving that the correlation is a between-group mixture effect of the persistent behavioural class rather than a byproduct of household size or geography.
5. **A methodological point worth carrying into the paper.** Round 10's "the events world cannot be matched" and note 57's reading both came from reading a moment k and not re-reading the same runs on the likelihood scale. The two estimators order these arms differently: on the moment scale world 3b is the most dispersed arm in this note (`k_mom = 0.152`), on the likelihood scale it is the least dispersed of the four matched worlds (`k_ml = 0.330`). Any claim about what a mechanism "can reach" should name the estimator.
