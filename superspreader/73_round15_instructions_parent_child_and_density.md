**Why these runs.** The comparison of the paper with the wider modelling literature left two checks that only the simulator can answer. First, a database of published transmission trees (Taube et al. 2022) reports that superspreaders infect other superspreaders more often than chance in 12 of 18 trees. The three worlds predict different things (no inheritance of offspring apart from shared settings in world 1, correlation through a persistent class of venue-goers in world 2b, and an unknown pattern for closed-group events), and I have never measured it, because my files carry the number of offspring per index case and not who infected whom. Second, the finding that occupancy cannot generate overdispersion in this kernel (Section 5.1) was obtained with no person-level variation; I have not asked what density-dependent transmission does in the worlds that do reproduce the published k, and one mobility-network model (Chang et al. 2020) relies on a density-dependent law. Run C answers the first and run D the second.

**Done on the drafting side, no run needed (for your information).** (i) Retrospective tracing yield from the delivered histograms: backward tracing finds an index case's infector and that infector's other offspring, forward tracing finds its own offspring; the ratio of backward to forward yield is (1 + E[Z(Z-1)]/E[Z])/E[Z] = 1/R + E[Z(Z-1)]/R^2, with Z the offspring count of an index case and R its mean, so it is fixed by the first two moments (the moment k) and not by the likelihood k; for the B1 cells it comes to 5.9, 7.5 and 8.3 for worlds 1, 2b and 3b, and for the B2 cells (extended-search cell for world 3b) to 6.0, 8.3 and 10.4; random detection leaves it unchanged. **Statistics to compute** (I will recompute them from the infection files; please report yours so the two can be compared).

1. **Parent and child association.** Parents are the index cases (infected on days 1 to 10 with a known infector). A child is an infection whose infector is a parent. For every (parent, child) pair report the Pearson and Spearman correlation of `offspring_14d` of the parent and of the child, pooled over replicates, with a 90% replicate-bootstrap interval. Report the mean `offspring_14d` of the children by parent offspring class (1, 2 to 5, 6 to 9, 10 or more), beside the mean `offspring_14d` of all infections occurring on the same days as those children (the day-matched expectation), and their ratio.
2. **Superspreader pairs** (the analogue of the statistic of Taube et al. 2022). A superspreader is an infection with `offspring_14d` above the 99th percentile of a Poisson distribution whose mean is the cell's mean offspring of index cases (the definition of Lloyd-Smith et al. 2005). Report the observed number of (superspreader parent, superspreader child) pairs, the number expected under independence (pairs with a superspreader parent times the share of children that are superspreaders) and their ratio, pooled and per replicate.
3. **Where the child was infected.** The same two statistics separately for children infected in the parent's household and children infected elsewhere, and for world 3b separately for children infected inside and outside events.
4. **Siblings.** The correlation of `offspring_14d` among children of the same parent (intraclass correlation, parents with at least two children) and, for parents with at least six offspring, the share of the parent's children infected in the single venue-day that holds the most of them (this repeats the venue-day share of the ten largest clusters, as a check of the export).
5. **Class membership** (world 2b): the share of children who are class members, by whether the parent is a class member, against the class share of the population; and the share of all infections that are class members' offspring.
6. **Tracing yield from traces.** For each index case report the number of siblings (other offspring of its infector) and the number of its own offspring; I will compare the mean sibling count plus one with the moment-based value above.

## Run D: the worlds under density-dependent transmission

The density exponent alpha replaces the occupancy N in the denominator of the kernel by N to the power alpha (alpha = 1 is the frequency-dependent default; alpha = 0 is mass action; Section 5.1, Table 5.1b). **Cells.** For each of worlds 1, 2b and 3b at the B1 parameter values (above), set alpha to 0.5 and to 0, keep the mechanism parameter fixed and recalibrate only the background coefficient to day 28 within 3% of 6,211.25 (up to four trials of 6 replicates on calibration seeds 76001 to 76006, then 12 replicates on seeds 77001 to 77012). Six cells. Starting values from Table 5.1b of the no-multiplier cells (rough, since the worlds have other sources): coefficient about 0.13 times the alpha = 1 value at alpha = 0.5, and about 0.0077 times at alpha = 0. In world 3b the event hazard may not depend on the coefficient; say how the event kernel treats alpha (event factor applied to the same occupancy-dependent hazard, or to a fixed hazard) and keep it as in the code. If a flag combination is not supported, report it and skip that cell.

**For every cell report** the likelihood k, moment k, ratio, observed zero share, share one to five, above five, maximum, observed minus negative-binomial zero share, 80% share, home share, household secondary attack rate, the ten-largest-cluster statistics, the median and 90th percentile of occupancy at the moment of infection, and the share of infections in the busiest 10% of venues (by infections), beside the alpha = 1 B1 cell of the same world.

**Registered predictions for run D.** (a) World 1 at alpha = 0: likelihood k between 0.15 and 0.23; the composition rule of Section 5.15 with the occupancy source of Table 5.1b gives about 0.18. (b) World 2b at alpha = 0: likelihood k lower than at alpha = 1 by between 15% and 45%. (c) World 3b at alpha = 0: likelihood k within 20% of its alpha = 1 value, because the events carry 70% of early offspring at B1. (d) At alpha = 0 the venue-day share of the ten largest clusters stays at 80% or more in world 3b, and the household share of the ten largest clusters of world 1 stays above that of worlds 2b and 3b. (e) The share of infections in the busiest 10% of venues rises with falling alpha in every world, and at alpha = 0 it exceeds 50% in at least one world (the mobility-network model of Chang et al. found 85% in Chicago, for a different law and with real venue sizes). Failure of (a) would mean that the composition rule does not extend to occupancy; I want that reported, not repaired.
