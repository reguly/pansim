## Wrong or ambiguous

1. **Cost estimate is far too high.** One 4-week replicate takes 13 to 20 s alone and about 115 s when 12 run at once on this 16-core machine (memory about 0.9 GB per run, which is the binding limit: 12 concurrent runs use 11 GB of 15.8 GB). That is about 10 s of wall clock per replicate. N1 items 1 and 2 (four worlds, calibration of 3 to 4 iterations of up to 12 replicates, plus a frozen cell each) are about 1 hour, not 3.5; the whole round is about 4 to 5 hours, not 12, with N0 and N4 taking minutes (N0: 20 s for all 1,152 replicates; N4: about 1 minute).
2. **N0 "day 56, 84 and 112 where run".** The 12-replicate arms are all 4-week runs, so those columns are only filled for the seven 16-week pair arms (8 replicates each). I export them in the same file. "Early R" and "mean offspring" are two definitions in the delivered files: early R is the mean 14-day offspring of the index cases (days 1 to 10); the all-node mean offspring of `r6.all_node_mean` is a different quantity. I give both (`early_R`, `mean_offspring_all_nodes`).
3. **"Top 1% infectors by agent type, pooled threshold".** The delivered files take the top 1% of each replicate separately. With the pooled threshold, ties at the threshold (offspring 12 in world 1) would add dozens of infectors (177 against 136), so I take exactly round(1% of the pooled index cases) with ties broken by a fixed random order, and write the threshold (smallest included offspring count). Column `top1pct_threshold_pooled`.
4. **Checkpoint rule has almost no power.** With 2 seed sets (round-12 set and 78001 to 78012) the between-set SD has 1 degree of freedom: its 90% interval runs from about 0.5 to 30 times its true value. "At most 1.2 times the within-set standard error for all worlds and statistics" is 16 comparisons (4 worlds, 4 statistics) each with one degree of freedom, so it will fail by chance alone even if the between-set variance is zero (for a pure-noise case, the chance that one comparison has SD at most 1.2 within-set SE is about 0.7 to 0.8, so all 16 pass with probability well below 5%). So item 3 (seed set 79001 to 79012) will in practice always run. I would run it regardless and pool the variance over worlds (the same ratio per statistic over four worlds has 4 degrees of freedom with 2 sets, 8 with 3). I will compute the checkpoint as you defined it and also the pooled ratio.
5. **N1 prediction (a) is not testable with two sets.** The ratio of between-set SD to within-set SE for one world needs at least 3 sets to be even rough. With 2 sets I report the absolute difference over its standard error (|d| over the combined SE), which is the z of note 64, and the SD only after item 3. World 3b has 4 sets already.
6. **N1 item 1 for worlds 1 and 2a/2b is a recalibration from the round-12 value.** The calibration starts from the round-12 coefficient (4 replicates for the pilot, then 12 as in round 12). If the round-12 coefficient is already within 3% on the new seeds, `match` stops there and the "calibrated" and "frozen" cells are the same coefficient. I run both anyway; when the coefficient is identical the two cells are repeat runs of one configuration and tell whether the simulator is deterministic (result: for world 1 both cells are identical to the last digit, so the simulator is deterministic for equal inputs).
7. **Seed set 78001 to 78012 is not new for worlds 1, 2a and 2b.** Seeds 78001 to 78008 were used in the 16-week repeat-infection runs of rounds 10 and 11 (worlds 1, 2a and the round-10 gate cell). They are different runs (vaccination off, episode trace, 16 weeks), but the seed numbers coincide. I do not expect this to matter and note it.
8. **N2 power.** One replicate set (12) gives a 90% half-width of about 0.011 on likelihood k (about 0.007 standard error). A one-sided change of 10% in the coefficient gives a difference of two such estimates with standard error about 0.01, so the thresholds 0.02 and 0.03 are about 2 to 3 standard errors; and the two runs are not paired (the trajectories differ from the first day). I will report both differences (0.9 against 1.0 and 1.1 against 1.0) and the central slope (1.1 against 0.9) with their standard errors, which is a better estimate than either one-sided change.
9. **N3 is more than "three secant iterations".** Each trial value of the shape or event rate changes the day-28 count, so a trial needs its own coefficient calibration (several runs of 6 replicates) before the likelihood k can be read at a comparable day 28. I count a "secant iteration" as one trial of the tuned parameter with the coefficient calibrated on the 6-replicate set. Likelihood k from 6 replicates has a standard error of about 0.015 to 0.02 (twice that of 12), so a target of 0.20 or 0.50 can only be met to about plus or minus 0.03; I report the achieved k on the 12 replicates. For world 2b ("multiplier shape with the venue factor fixed at 10") I read the venue factor as the scene parameter lambda = 10 of the round-11 arm. World 3b at k 0.50 needs a lower event rate than the round-12 rate (0.1456); it is not obvious that the base k reaches 0.50 before the event mechanism disappears.
10. **Prediction N3(c) "stays at least 3 points below the negative binomial zero share at k 0.50"** depends on the event rate that gives k 0.50; if that rate is small, event offspring are few and 3 points is likely out of reach. That is a result, not a defect.
